Data & method

Data preparation and RoBERTa feature fusion

Normalization creates consistent views of product fields. A 42-dimensional comparison vector records agreement, differences and missing values; RoBERTa encodes both listing orders. Development and calibration data select the checkpoint and decision cutoff.

C+ names the final model in this project: RoBERTa with 42 comparison features, averaged across three training seeds.

Model card

C+ names the final model in this project: RoBERTa with 42 comparison features, averaged across three training seeds.

Input

A pair of retailer listings. Title, brand, native model number and category form the text input; description, price, currency and missing-field states contribute through comparison features.

Architecture

RoBERTa-base jointly encodes the pair and projects its 768-dimensional text representation to 64 dimensions. This is concatenated with 42 comparison features. A 106 → 64 → 1 classifier produces a raw score (logit); training updates both encoder and classifier.

Scoring and decision

Within each seed, average logits for the AB and BA record orders, then apply sigmoid to obtain a probability. Average probabilities equally across seeds 42, 43, 44. A final score at or above 0.3947772259513537 is classified as a match.

01

Field normalization, quality states and title IDF

Input

Two supplied retailer records with title, brand, description, price and priceCurrency; native modelno and category fields.

Operation

Create normalized business and native views. Apply Unicode normalization, case folding and whitespace cleanup to text. Parse plain nonnegative prices, retain missing/ambiguous states, and enable price comparison only for equal recognized currencies.

Output

A pair record with pair_id, left, right, left_native and right_native, including explicit price/currency quality states. The original source is not replaced by a guessed value.

Reason for the choice

Title formatting varies, while a model digit or currency difference can change what is being compared. Separating normalized values from unavailable evidence prevents missing prices or two absent brands from becoming positive equality evidence.

  • Missing fields

    Text comparison uses Unicode normalization, case folding and whitespace cleanup. Missing brand, description, model and category values remain missing; the feature vector records presence indicators where defined. Two absent values are not counted as a positive equality comparison.

  • Price and currency comparability

    A plain nonnegative decimal can be parsed as a price. Ambiguous symbols or separators keep an unresolved quality state. Price similarity is enabled only when both prices parse and their recognized currency codes agree; otherwise the comparable-price indicator is zero. No exchange-rate conversion or price imputation is applied.

  • Title and native model codes

    Model-like title codes are extracted and compared as sets. Features describe exact overlap, normalized edit similarity and shared letters with different digits or shared digits with different letters. Native model numbers are compared separately and checked against the opposite listing’s title codes.

  • Title IDF fitted on fit records

    Title word and character document frequencies are fitted on content-deduplicated business records from fit only. The saved IDF state weights title overlap in later roles without refitting. Direct title-number and compact-title comparisons are also included; they do not require fitting. For example, in 100 synthetic training records, a term appearing in 80 of those records has weight 1 + ln(101/81) ≈ 1.221; one appearing in two records has weight 1 + ln(101/3) ≈ 4.517. Common terms therefore contribute less evidence of overlap. These counts illustrate the calculation; they are not dataset statistics.

02

Fit, development, calibration and evaluation roles

Input

Frozen pair membership and the corresponding fit, dev, calibration and evaluation records; separate non-fit label lists.

Operation

Fit model parameters and title IDF on fit. Select one common ensemble epoch on dev. Select a cutoff on calibration. Apply the selected settings to evaluation. Join non-fit labels by pair_id only in the relevant metric or selection command.

Output

Prepared role-specific inputs and separate selection/calibration/evaluation labels. The reported evaluation pool has 555 pairs, including 190 matches.

Reason for the choice

Epoch selection and cutoff selection answer different procedural questions. Keeping them separate prevents the current prediction interface from consuming evaluation labels. It does not establish that the retrospective pool was untouched earlier; its historical exposure record remains incomplete.

The pairs have four preassigned project roles. Fit is used to estimate model parameters and title IDF; development selects an epoch; calibration selects a cutoff; evaluation supplies the reported comparison. These assignments do not establish an untouched official test split or an entity-disjoint evaluation.

03

The ordered 42-feature vector

Input

Normalized business/native pair views and title IDF fitted on deduplicated fit records.

Operation

Compute 14 business comparisons, 8 title-IDF/direct-title comparisons, 8 title-code comparisons and 12 native-model/category comparisons. Preserve the FEATURES.json order and reuse the fit IDF state for later roles.

Output

One finite x42 vector in [0, 1] per pair, with similarity, presence, disagreement and comparability values. IDs and target labels are not vector entries.

Reason for the choice

The vector gives the model explicit observations such as a shared model code, a changed digit, a missing brand or an incomparable price. The fixed order makes the same meaning available to the tabular references and the neural fusion head.

Preparation produces one x42 vector per pair. Its four groups have 14, 8, 8 and 12 values, in the exact order recorded in FEATURES.json. IDs and labels are excluded. Similarity values for missing inputs are zero, with separate presence or comparability indicators where defined. Expand a group to inspect its identifiers and descriptions.

04

RoBERTa text encoding and feature-fusion head

Input

Brand, native model number, category and title serialized in AB and BA order, plus the pair’s x42 vector.

Operation

Encode each order with RoBERTa-base within the 256-token limit. Project the 768-dimensional first-token representation to 64 GELU units, concatenate 42 comparisons, then apply a 64-unit GELU layer, dropout 0.1 and a linear output.

Output

One scalar logit for AB and one for BA in each seed. Description and price evidence reach the head through x42, rather than the text serialization.

Reason for the choice

The text branch represents the pair’s language, while explicit field comparisons retain model-code and missingness information. This is the tested fusion structure. The near-equal C0 AP limits the feature-contribution conclusion supported by this pool.

Each seed uses the same architecture with its own trained parameters. The text branch receives brand, native model number, category and title. The comparison branch supplies x42, including description and price evidence. The fusion head returns one logit for each listing order; aggregation produces the final pair score.

05

Direction averaging and three-seed probability ensemble

Input

AB and BA logits from seeds 42, 43, 44, all using the common checkpoint selected at epoch 4.

Operation

Within each seed, average the two direction logits and apply sigmoid. Then average the three resulting probabilities with equal weights. Direction averaging precedes sigmoid; seed averaging follows it.

Output

One continuous C+ score for each pair_id. This score is later ranked or compared with the calibration cutoff.

Reason for the choice

Processing both listing orders makes the score symmetric to swapping the inputs. Averaging three fixed seeds defines the reported ensemble without selecting the most favorable seed on evaluation labels.

Within each seed, average the AB and BA logits, then apply sigmoid. Average the resulting probabilities across seeds 42, 43 and 44. The order is fixed: do not average all logits together or choose one seed by its score.

pₛ = sigmoid((zₛ,AB + zₛ,BA) / 2)

p = (p₄₂ + p₄₃ + p₄₄) / 3

Training follows the six-epoch schedule; development data selects the shared epoch-4 checkpoints. The ensemble score is classified using the calibration cutoff 0.3947772259513537: scores at or above it are matches. The cutoff is not 0.5 and is not reselected on evaluation data.

06

Fixed training settings and selected checkpoint

Input

Calibration scores and labels, followed by evaluation scores and separately supplied evaluation labels.

Operation

Choose maximum calibration F1, then precision, then the higher cutoff. Freeze cutoff 0.3947772259513537 for C+. Align evaluation scores and labels by pair_id, compute AP with score ties grouped, P@100 with pair_id tie ordering, and the integer confusion matrix.

Output

C+ AP 0.9660, F1 0.9016 and P@100 1.00 on 555 pairs; TP 165, FP 11, FN 25 and TN 354.

Reason for the choice

AP measures ranking, while F1 evaluates decisions at the selected cutoff and P@100 describes only the first 100 pairs. These results belong to the fixed retrospective pool. They do not establish independent new Walmart–Amazon sample performance, and replay agreement is not fresh training or a new evaluation.
