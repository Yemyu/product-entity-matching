Data & method

Data preparation and RoBERTa feature fusion

Follow the product pairs from their project data roles through field normalization, explicit comparisons and joint RoBERTa encoding. The model scores both listing orders and combines three training seeds; development and calibration data choose the checkpoint and cutoff.

C+ names the final model in this project: RoBERTa with 42 comparison features, averaged across three training seeds.

Model card

C+ names the final model in this project: RoBERTa with 42 comparison features, averaged across three training seeds.

Input

Two retailer records. Title, brand, native model number and category form the text input; description, price, currency and missing-field states contribute through comparison features.

Architecture

RoBERTa jointly encodes the pair, projects its 768-dimensional representation to 64 values and joins 42 comparisons. The 106 → 64 → 1 classifier returns a raw score, called a logit.

Scoring and decision

AB and BA are the two listing orders. Within each seed, average their logits and apply sigmoid. Average the resulting probabilities across seeds 42, 43, 44. A final score at or above 0.3947772259513537 is classified as a match.

01

Data source and project roles

The experiment uses a custom fixed set of product pairs from Walmart and Amazon. A pair contains two retailer records, and its label indicates whether they refer to the same product. The reported evaluation pool contains 555 pairs: 190 matches and 365 nonmatches.

The four roles below are project assignments. Fit estimates model parameters and title IDF (inverse document frequency), which downweights words that occur in many training titles. Development selects one shared checkpoint epoch, calibration selects a decision cutoff, and evaluation supplies the reported comparison.

Fit

3,738

Fit title IDF, the numerical-feature references and the models for each training seed.

Development

199

Use the three seeds’ predictions to select one common epoch.

Calibration

209

Select a cutoff by calibration F1, then precision, then the higher cutoff.

Evaluation

555

Apply the saved cutoff and report ranking metrics and prediction counts.

Only fit inputs include target labels. Other roles store labels separately and join them to scores by pair_id in selection or metric commands; preparation reuses the saved fit IDF state. These input rules do not establish an untouched official test split or an entity-disjoint evaluation. The fixed pool’s historical exposure record is incomplete.

02

Field normalization and title weighting

Preparation keeps normalized business fields and native model/category fields in separate views. It produces comparable values without replacing missing or ambiguous source values. The rules below are shared by the numerical-feature models and the neural model. A token is a text fragment produced by a tokenizer; the feature calculations below use their own tokenization rules.

  • Missing fields

    Text comparison uses Unicode normalization, case folding and whitespace cleanup. Missing brand, description, model and category values remain missing; the feature vector records presence indicators where defined. Two absent values are not counted as a positive equality comparison.

  • Price and currency comparability

    A plain nonnegative decimal can be parsed as a price. Ambiguous symbols or separators keep an unresolved quality state. Price similarity is enabled only when both prices parse and their recognized currency codes agree; otherwise the comparable-price indicator is zero. No exchange-rate conversion or price imputation is applied.

  • Title and native model codes

    Model-like title codes are extracted and compared as sets. Features describe exact overlap, normalized edit similarity and shared letters with different digits or shared digits with different letters. Native model numbers are compared separately and checked against the opposite listing’s title codes.

  • Title IDF fitted on fit records

    Title word and character document frequencies are fitted on content-deduplicated business records from fit only. The saved IDF state weights title overlap in later roles without refitting. Direct title-number and compact-title comparisons are also included; they do not require fitting. For example, in 100 synthetic training records, a term appearing in 80 of those records has weight 1 + ln(101/81) ≈ 1.221; one appearing in two records has weight 1 + ln(101/3) ≈ 4.517. Common terms therefore contribute less evidence of overlap. These counts illustrate the calculation; they are not dataset statistics.

03

The ordered 42-feature vector

Each pair receives a 42-dimensional comparison vector, named x42 in the code. Its four groups contain 14, 8, 8 and 12 values in the FEATURES.json order. IDs and labels are excluded. Similarities for missing inputs are zero, with separate presence or comparability indicators where defined. Expand a group to inspect the identifiers and meanings.

Fourteen values compare title and description tokens, brand agreement, numeric tokens, title length and prices. Presence and price-comparability indicators distinguish unavailable evidence from a measured similarity.

04

RoBERTa text encoding and feature-fusion head

The model combines a joint text representation with the x42 comparison vector. AB means listing A followed by B; BA reverses that order. Each training seed has its own fitted parameters and scores both orders with the same architecture.

Text length and field token budgets

A token is a text unit produced by the model’s tokenizer. Each record has budgets of 16 tokens for brand, 32 for native model number, 16 for category and 62 for title. A long title retains its first 40 and last 22 tokens. The two records use at most 252 tokens; four special tokens bring the pair limit to 256.

05

Direction averaging and the three-seed ensemble

A logit is the classifier’s raw score before conversion to a probability. Within each seed, average the AB and BA logits, then apply sigmoid to map the result into 0–1. Take the equal mean of those probabilities across seeds 42, 43, 44. This gives a score symmetric to exchanging the two listings, while preserving the distinction between averaging logits and averaging probabilities.

pₛ = sigmoid((zₛ,AB + zₛ,BA) / 2)

p = mean(p₄₂, p₄₃, p₄₄)

All three seeds use the common epoch 4 selected on development data. The final score is classified as a match when it is at or above the calibration cutoff 0.3947772259513537.

06

Checkpoint selection and calibration

Training follows a six-epoch schedule. Development compares ensemble predictions and selects common epoch 4 for seeds 42, 43, 44. Calibration selects cutoff 0.3947772259513537, which is then reused for evaluation.

Calibration maximizes F1, breaking ties by precision and then the higher cutoff. Evaluation labels are kept separate from model inputs and are not used to choose these settings. The table below records the settings of the reported experiment.
