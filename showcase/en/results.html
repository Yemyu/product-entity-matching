Fixed-pool comparison

Retrospective results on 555 product pairs

Six methods are compared on the same 555 product pairs. The final model C+ combines RoBERTa with comparison features; C0 is an independently trained control with those features set to zero. Compare ranking quality, calibrated decisions and errors below.

C+ names the final model in this project: RoBERTa with 42 comparison features, averaged across three training seeds.

01

AP ranking and F1 at calibrated cutoffs

All six methods score the same 555-pair retrospective pool, including 190 matches. Average precision (AP) measures ranking from continuous scores; F1 combines precision and recall at each method’s own calibration cutoff. Both plot axes cover 0–1.

The six methods being compared

B22
Logistic regression on the first 22 comparison features.
L30
Gradient-boosted trees on 30 comparison features.
S33
L30 plus three fixed MiniLM text-similarity and missingness features.
L42
Gradient-boosted trees on all 42 comparisons; the strongest numerical-feature reference on this pool.
C+
RoBERTa pair encoding with 42 comparisons, averaged across three training seeds.
C0
A RoBERTa control with the comparison-feature input set to zero.

C0 is trained independently of C+, with all 42 comparison values zero throughout training and inference. It retains the neural dimensions and uses the six-epoch scheduler for four training epochs; the comparison uses epoch 4.

05

Metric definitions and cutoff dependence

AP

Average precision (AP) summarizes precision over the ranking of positive pairs. It uses the continuous pair scores and groups tied scores. AP 0.9660 is not a classification accuracy of 0.9660.

F1

F1 combines precision and recall after turning scores into match/nonmatch predictions at a cutoff. The cutoff is selected on calibration labels and fixed before evaluation. Changing it changes the balance of false matches and missed matches.

P@100

P@100 is the fraction of actual matches among the first 100 ranked pairs. Score ties at the boundary are ordered by pair_id. P@100 1.00 describes those 100 pairs in this pool, rather than all products or future listings.

06

Evaluation scope and limitations

The 555-pair evaluation pool is retrospective and its historical use is not fully documented. The current label-separation interface does not resolve that history. An independent new Walmart–Amazon sample or an entity-disjoint evaluation has not been established.

C+ improves AP and F1 over L42 on this pool, but its AP is only 0.0381 percentage points above independently trained C0. The aggregate results do not establish a substantial feature-driven ranking gain or statistical significance; no confidence intervals are available.

Existing-weight replay verifies artifact preparation and prediction through the public code entry. It does not reproduce fresh end-to-end training or supply new unseen-data evidence. Production latency and business benefits have not been measured, and pair scores alone do not justify automatic catalog merging.
